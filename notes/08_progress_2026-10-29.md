## Experiments and findings

### E31: read-depth ablation, human (in paper)
Nested subsampling d1-d32. TransDecoder has the best overall exact F1 at every level (0.844 -> 0.806).
Per CDS type: classifiers lead on complete and 3' partial CDSs, Prodigal and the longest ORF lead on
internal CDSs, and TD leads on 5' partials at d1-d4.

### E32: simulated Arabidopsis assembly, d1-d32 (complete; not in paper)
- **Inputs:** TAIR12 truth, ERR4140056 leaf expression. Noncoding transcripts get only 0.4% of reads, so
  the series tests frame and end placement rather than noncoding rejection. Negatives fall from 1,372
  (d1) to 74 (d32).
- **Ab initio TD** (exact F1 0.881 -> 0.828) leads overall down to d8. At d16-d32 the longest ORF (0.852,
  0.876) and Prodigal pass it, because internal CDSs reach 49-57% of the truth.
- **With homology and the start assist,** TD leads at every depth (0.899-0.915; E33 "Arabidopsis depth
  series").

### E33: keep-open vs trim for 5'-open ORFs
- **Steps 1-2 (not adopted).** Classifiers on features of the open segment and its context: the
  self-trained ones did not beat the release rule.
- **Step 3 -> adopted in PyTD a5b24c3.** Homology rules:
  - R2 trims when the best hit's subject N-terminus lies at the ATG;
  - R1 keeps the end open when the alignment starts upstream of the ATG;
  - R3 keeps it open when a Pfam envelope starts upstream of the ATG.
  - If trim and keep rules conflict, or none fires, the ab initio rule decides. Each rule is used only
    when its evidence type was supplied. The assist is on by default; opt out with
    `--no-homology-start-assist`.
- **Gains:**
  - benchmark fragments: median +5.9 exact F1 points (none down);
  - complete inputs: about 0 (worst -0.58, *T. thermophila*);
  - simulated human d1/d8/d32: +2.0/+5.4/+9.0;
  - Arabidopsis d1-d32: +2.3 to +7.2 over retention-only homology;
  - GM12878 Trinity: MANE exact 7,063 -> 7,190, and true 5' partials trimmed to an internal ATG 328 -> 52.
- **Step 4 (not adopted).** Neither top-n hits, all HSPs, `--more-sensitive` nor e-values from 1e-10 to 10
  improve on top-1 at 1e-5 (at best +0.1-0.2 points held out).
  - Most remaining errors are missed CDSs, not start decisions.
  - "Hit, no rule fired" is the main residual decision error: the alignment starts inside the subject.
- **Step 5 (not adopted).** UniRef50 vs Swiss-Prot:
  - better for clades Swiss-Prot covers poorly (athal, dmel, protists);
  - worse in human and mouse (unreviewed orthologs with longer N-termini; lncRNA calls 3,268 -> 8,220);
  - 21x slower.
  - Swiss-Prot stays recommended. UniRef90 was not tested.

### E34: anatomy of missed CDSs (diagnostic; not in paper)
- **What the misses are.** 89-99.6% of the CDSs with no prediction on the simulated assemblies have a
  candidate that fails the coding test.
  - They are short (median ~70-80 codons) and weakly coding: about 40% of the per-base frame contrast of
    found CDSs of the same length.
  - Human: mostly short alternative-isoform CDSs.
  - Arabidopsis: main proteins whose GC3 lies outside the training set's range.
- **Fixes tested.**
  - Homology halves the Arabidopsis misses.
  - Relaxing the q-value cutoff costs about one lncRNA call per CDS recovered on human d1.
  - A lower minimum length recovers almost nothing.
- **5'-open candidates whose correct answer is "trim":** kept open in 10-16% (human) and 15-36% (athal)
  of cases.
  - 56-70% of these: the UTR segment scores as coding.
  - 19-42%: a negative Kozak score vetoes the trim. This matters more in athal, whose Kozak contexts are
    weaker.
  - 2-5% (human) and under 0.5% (athal): the true start is not the first ATG.
